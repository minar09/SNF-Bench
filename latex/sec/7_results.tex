\subsection{Native-Capability Audit}
\label{sec:results_native}

Table~\ref{tab:t2v_audit} summarizes SNF-Bench measurements under released inference configurations at the two principal horizons, with all four reported in the supplementary material. The audited systems happen to share a deployment envelope---all seven generate $832\times480$ at $16$\,fps and all reach the $240$\,s horizon without modification to their released temporal policy---so no per-system configuration table is needed; where a system could not reach a horizon, the entry would be marked unsupported rather than populated with altered settings.

\paragraph{Static fidelity.} fBD captures accumulated geometric displacement; NBF captures residual instantaneous motion inside $\Omega_{\mathrm{static}}$. The two are not redundant: a system can hold its structure in place while its static region shimmers, and the reverse. Across the \SNFnPublicTTV{} audited systems at 60\,s the two agree on the extremes and diverge in the middle of the field, so both are reported rather than one standing for the other.

\paragraph{Motion persistence.} Dynamic-region behavior is evaluated at the joint operating point $\bigl(\mathrm{MCFF}(\mathcal{L}),\,\mathrm{FP}\bigr)$, never FP alone: sustained flow requires non-trivial late motion \emph{and} high retention. Reporting MCFF-E beside MCFF-L makes the degenerate case visible---a system with negligible motion throughout cannot reach a high FP without also displaying a small MCFF-E.

\paragraph{Static--dynamic operating regimes.} Figure~\ref{fig:regimes_native} places each system in the static-drift--surviving-flow plane, exposing qualitatively different regimes---low-drift/low-motion, high-motion/high-drift, and low-drift/non-trivial-motion---without collapsing the axes into a scalar score. The two extremes are informative in opposite directions: Infinite-Forcing attains the lowest static drift of any audited system while placing last on surviving late motion, and Causal-Forcing sits alone at the opposite corner.

\subsection{Drift Leakage Changes the Interpretation of Apparent Motion}
\label{sec:results_drift}

We ask whether large apparent motion reflects intended dynamic-region activity or coherent scene displacement, considering whole-frame motion scores jointly with NBF, DLR and DAR. A generic motion score answers whether substantial image motion exists; SNF-Bench asks where in the frame it originates and how much survives compensation for background displacement. A method may therefore exhibit both a high whole-frame motion score and a high DLR---not a contradiction, but a different interpretation of the same motion.

The effect is systematic rather than anecdotal. Over the audited systems at 60\,s, VBench Dynamic Degree correlates with SNF-Bench's static-region flow at Spearman $\rho = \SNFddNBF$ (Table~\ref{tab:disagreement}): the systems a generic metric reads as most dynamic are, to a close approximation, the systems whose backgrounds move most. Figure~\ref{fig:rank_disagreement} shows the consequence for ordering at every horizon. Causal-Forcing ranks \SNFrankCFDD{} of \SNFnPublicTTV{} on Dynamic Degree and \SNFrankCFNBF{} of \SNFnPublicTTV{} on NBF; Infinite-Forcing occupies the mirror position, ranking \SNFrankIFNBF{} on static fidelity and \SNFrankIFDD{} on apparent motion. Read jointly with its magnitudes, Causal-Forcing's DLR of \SNFvalCFDLR{} indicates static-region motion energy almost equal to that of the region intended to move, while its DAR of \SNFvalCFDAR{} indicates that a rigid global model accounts for only part of it---the remainder is non-rigid instability, which a translation-only account would have misdescribed.

\subsection{Category and Horizon Dependence}
\label{sec:results_categories}

Aggregates can conceal scene dependence, so all headline numbers are macro-averaged over the \SNFnCategories{} flow-medium categories of Sec.~\ref{sec:benchmark} rather than averaged over prompts directly. This matters: the 60\,s prompt set is \SNFtopCatShare\% channel water, so a prompt-level mean is substantially a measurement of one medium. Complete per-category values appear in the supplementary material, together with the category balance of every track and horizon.

Horizon dependence is read from the per-horizon panels of Table~\ref{tab:t2v_audit}. A useful long-horizon generator should not merely score well at one duration but remain comparatively stable as the horizon grows. The 5\,s tier serves as an initialization sanity check; the 240\,s tier is exploratory and determines no principal conclusion, but it is reported because the ordering it induces is itself evidence about metric behavior under extended rollout.

\subsection{Deployment Sensitivity}
\label{sec:results_deployment}

Native capability is finally compared against the matched stress setting via the per-checkpoint change $\Delta m = m_{\mathrm{matched}} - m_{\mathrm{native}}$ for the checkpoints evaluated under both regimes; absolute matched scores are not interpreted as the published method's performance. Table~\ref{tab:deployment_sensitivity} reports representative factors. Large changes indicate sensitivity to temporal block size, cache policy, or denoising schedule; small changes indicate that static--dynamic behavior transfers across deployment regimes. The audit establishes \emph{that} behavior changes under the matched regime; attributing a change to a specific architectural mechanism requires method-specific intervention beyond SNF-Bench's scope.

\subsection{Summary of Audit Findings}
\label{sec:results_summary}

Answering the four pre-specified questions of Sec.~\ref{sec:audit_questions}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Static fidelity.} Audited systems separate cleanly on fBD and NBF, and the separation widens with horizon. No system holds its static support fixed while also sustaining dynamic-region motion at the level it produces early in the rollout.
\item \textbf{Motion persistence.} Read jointly, MCFF-E, MCFF-L and FP distinguish sustained flow from two different degenerate cases---motion that decays, and motion that was never present---which FP alone conflates.
\item \textbf{Metric interpretation.} Whole-frame motion and static-region drift induce near-opposite orderings ($\rho = \SNFddNBF$ against NBF), and the inversion is stable across horizons. This is the benchmark's principal finding.
\item \textbf{Deployment sensitivity.} Static--dynamic behavior is configuration-dependent, so a single number attached to a method name is under-specified without its deployment regime.
\end{enumerate}
No result subsection is added or removed based on which system performs best.
