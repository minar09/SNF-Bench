\paragraph{Static fidelity.} fBD captures accumulated geometric displacement; NBF captures residual instantaneous motion inside $\Omega_{\mathrm{static}}$. The analysis reports (i) which comparison groups exhibit reliably lower fBD/NBF at 60\,s and 120\,s, (ii) whether static fidelity degrades consistently with horizon, and (iii) whether failures concentrate in particular categories. Differences not separated by paired-bootstrap uncertainty are not treated as meaningful.

\paragraph{Motion persistence.} Dynamic-region behavior is evaluated at the joint operating point $\bigl(\mathrm{MCFF}(\mathcal{L}),\,\mathrm{FP}\bigr)$, never FP alone: sustained flow requires non-trivial late motion \emph{and} high retention. The analysis distinguishes methods that (i) preserve substantial late motion, (ii) exhibit genuine decay, or (iii) appear stable primarily because little motion exists throughout.

\paragraph{Static--dynamic operating regimes.} Figure~\ref{fig:regimes_native} places each system in the static-fidelity--flow-persistence plane, exposing qualitatively different regimes---low-drift/low-motion, high-motion/high-drift, and low-drift/non-trivial-motion---without collapsing the axes into a scalar score.

\subsection{Drift Leakage Changes the Interpretation of Apparent Motion}
\label{sec:results_driftfrac}

We ask whether large apparent motion reflects intended dynamic-region activity or coherent scene displacement, considering whole-frame motion scores jointly with NBF, DLR and DAR. A generic motion score answers whether substantial image motion exists; SNF-Bench asks how much survives compensation for background displacement. A method may therefore exhibit both a high whole-frame motion score and a high DLR---not a contradiction, but a different interpretation of the same motion.

Table~\ref{tab:interpretation_changes} records interpretation changes meeting three pre-specified conditions: (1) generic and SNF measurements are computed on the \emph{same outputs}; (2) the relevant SNF metric passed the validation of Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}; and (3) the difference exceeds paired uncertainty rather than reflecting a small ordinal shift. \textbf{All cases satisfying these conditions are reported; none is selectively omitted.} The analysis is complementary: whole-frame metrics measure what they were designed to measure, and SNF-Bench exposes where that quantity spatially originates.

\subsection{Category and Horizon Dependence}
\label{sec:results_categories}

Aggregates can conceal scene dependence, so category-level measurements are reported for each factor (Fig.~\ref{fig:category_heatmap}; complete values in supplementary). Broad-area motion (ocean) stresses temporal memory differently from narrow directional flow (rivers), while rain, snow, and smoke create dense motion fields near static boundaries; a system occupying different static--dynamic regimes across categories is reported as such rather than averaged away. Horizon dependence is analyzed via $\Delta_{60\rightarrow120}\,m = m_{120} - m_{60}$ per method and category: a useful long-horizon generator should not merely score well at one duration but remain comparatively stable as the horizon grows. The 5\,s tier serves only as an initialization sanity check; the 240\,s tier is exploratory and determines no principal conclusion.

\subsection{Deployment Sensitivity}
\label{sec:results_deployment}

Native capability is finally compared against the matched stress setting via the per-checkpoint change $\Delta m = m_{\mathrm{matched}} - m_{\mathrm{native}}$ for directly comparable metrics; absolute matched scores are not interpreted as the published method's performance. Table~\ref{tab:deployment_sensitivity} reports representative factors (fBD, NBF, late MCFF, DLR). Large changes indicate sensitivity to temporal block size, cache policy, or denoising schedule; small changes indicate that static--dynamic behavior transfers across deployment regimes. The audit establishes \emph{that} behavior changes under the matched regime; attributing a change to a specific architectural mechanism requires method-specific intervention beyond SNF-Bench's scope.
