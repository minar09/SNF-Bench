\section{Public-Model Audit}
\label{sec:audit}

We apply SNF-Bench to publicly released long-horizon generators. The goal is not a universal leaderboard but a determination of how deployed systems occupy the static-fidelity/flow-persistence space when used as their authors intended.

\paragraph{Systems and setting.}
The audit covers \SNFnPublicTTV{} publicly released autoregressive or streaming generators with accessible checkpoints and reproducible inference code, frozen as a set before the sweep. Unpublished and internal systems are excluded by construction, as are systems whose outputs cannot be independently reproduced. Each is run in the deployment regime its release intends, answering a capability question: what behaviour does the released system exhibit when used as documented? Differences in temporal block size, denoising schedule, and memory policy are preserved deliberately---they are part of the released system, not nuisance variables---so the results compare deployed systems rather than isolated checkpoints under matched compute. The audited systems share a deployment envelope: all generate $832\times480$ at $16$\,fps and all reach the $240$\,s horizon without modification to their released temporal policy. A system unable to reach a horizon without altering a mechanism central to its released procedure would be marked unsupported there; we never substitute a block size, cache policy, or schedule to populate a table entry.

A secondary analysis evaluates checkpoints under a common long-horizon wrapper, measuring sensitivity to deployment regime rather than capability. Those scores are never used to redefine native behaviour or attributed to the published system, and are reported in the supplementary material.

\paragraph{Controls and statistics.}
Within each benchmark item every system receives identical conditioning, and the seed set is fixed before the sweep. Seed control guarantees reproducibility within a system; because implementations consume randomness differently, identical seeds do not induce matched noise draws across systems, so cross-system comparison rests on the paired-item design rather than on paired noise. Generated videos are retained in native form, and the normalisation of Sec.~\ref{sec:metrics} is applied unchanged to every system.

Scores are computed per item and macro-averaged across categories. Uncertainty is estimated by percentile bootstrap~\cite{efron1979bootstrap} over items, and we report $95\%$ intervals. Exact ordinal ranks are avoided where uncertainty does not support them: systems whose intervals overlap substantially are not described as separated. Operational limits---unsupported horizons, failed generations, memory exhaustion, invalid outputs---are reported as rates against the frozen manifest and never removed after inspection.

To test whether the decomposition changes interpretation, the same outputs are additionally scored with established whole-frame metrics and we compare the orderings they induce. The focus is interpretation change, not a claim that existing metrics are incorrect.
