\section{Public-Model Audit}
\label{sec:audit}

We apply SNF-Bench to publicly released long-horizon generators. The goal is not a universal leaderboard but a determination of how deployed systems occupy the static-fidelity/flow-persistence space under a single recorded deployment setting.

\paragraph{Systems and setting.}
The audit covers \SNFnPublicTTV{} publicly released autoregressive or streaming generators with accessible checkpoints and reproducible inference code, frozen as a set before the sweep. Unpublished and internal systems are excluded by construction, as are systems whose outputs cannot be independently reproduced. Every checkpoint runs through one common inference configuration rather than each method's released defaults: a four-step denoising schedule, guidance $5.0$, six frames per block and a fixed seed, with only the checkpoint and horizon varying. This is a \emph{matched-setting} comparison and we describe it as one. It isolates the checkpoint, which is what makes the systems comparable, but a method distilled for a different step budget is not seen at its own operating point, so results are statements about these checkpoints under this configuration. The audited systems share a deployment envelope---all generate $832\times480$ at $16$\,fps and reach $240$\,s without modification to their released temporal policy---so no comparison here is confounded by output format. We never substitute a block size, cache policy or schedule to populate a table entry; a system that cannot reach a horizon unmodified is marked unsupported.

The image-conditioned track is audited under the same protocol and the same frozen specification. Systems evaluated under a common long-horizon wrapper rather than their released configuration are labelled \emph{matched} in every table, and their scores are read as deployment sensitivity rather than as the published method's performance; we never rank a native row against a matched one.

\paragraph{Controls and statistics.}
Within each item every system receives identical conditioning, and the seed set is fixed before the sweep. Seed control guarantees reproducibility within a system, but because implementations consume randomness differently, identical seeds do not induce matched noise draws across systems; cross-system comparison therefore rests on the paired-item design. Videos are retained in native form and the normalisation of Sec.~\ref{sec:metrics} applied unchanged.

Scores are computed per item and averaged over items; the sensitivity of every conclusion to category macro-averaging is reported in the supplementary material. Uncertainty is estimated by percentile bootstrap~\cite{efron1979bootstrap} over items, and we report $95\%$ intervals. Exact ordinal ranks are avoided where uncertainty does not support them: systems whose intervals overlap substantially are not described as separated. Operational limits---unsupported horizons, failed generations, memory exhaustion, invalid outputs---are reported as rates against the frozen manifest and never removed after inspection.

To test whether the decomposition changes interpretation, the same outputs are additionally scored with established whole-frame metrics and the induced orderings compared. The focus is interpretation change, not a claim that existing metrics are wrong.
