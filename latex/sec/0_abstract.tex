\begin{abstract}
Long-horizon video generation is typically evaluated with whole-frame metrics that reward motion and temporal consistency. For fixed-camera nature scenes this creates an ambiguity: motion in water, fire, smoke, or rain is desirable, whereas motion of the background is a generation error. A model can thus score highly on motion while drifting, or on consistency while its flow stagnates. We introduce \textbf{SNF-Bench} (Static--Natural Flow), an evaluation framework for long-horizon fixed-camera nature-flow generation that separates \emph{static support} from \emph{dynamic flow} across text-to-video and image-to-video tracks at 60--120\,s horizons. Its protocol measures three factors: \textbf{Static Fidelity}, \textbf{Flow Persistence} (retention jointly with absolute magnitude), and \textbf{Drift Leakage}. Every measurement is validated mechanistically: we inject global translation, rotation, and scale drift and progressive motion attenuation at known severity into real fixed-camera clips, and require each factor to respond in its expected direction and to remain selective against unrelated corruptions. Auditing publicly released long-horizon generators under their intended configurations, we find that whole-frame motion and static-region drift induce near-opposite orderings of the same outputs, and that the same controlled drift which our static-fidelity factors detect \emph{increases} a widely used whole-frame motion score. SNF-Bench evaluates \emph{where} motion occurs and whether it persists---not whether fluid dynamics are physically realistic.
\end{abstract}
