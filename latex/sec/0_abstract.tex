\begin{abstract}
Long-horizon video generation is typically evaluated with whole-frame metrics that reward motion and temporal consistency. For fixed-camera nature scenes this creates an ambiguity: motion in water, fire, smoke, or rain is desirable, whereas motion of the background is a generation error. A model can thus score highly on motion while drifting, or on consistency while its flow stagnates. We introduce \textbf{SNF-Bench} (Static--Natural Flow), an evaluation framework for long-horizon fixed-camera nature-flow generation that separates \emph{static support} from \emph{dynamic flow} across text-to-video and image-to-video tracks at 60--120\,s horizons. Its protocol measures three factors: \textbf{Static Fidelity}, \textbf{Flow Persistence} (retention jointly with absolute magnitude), and \textbf{Drift Leakage}. All measurements are validated with controlled perturbations of known drift, motion attenuation, and generation-like corruptions, including calibration of drift leakage and mask-sensitivity analysis. Auditing public long-horizon generators under native and matched stress-test configurations, we find that whole-frame metrics and SNF-Bench yield materially different interpretations when high apparent motion co-occurs with background instability. SNF-Bench evaluates \emph{where} motion occurs and whether it persists---not whether fluid dynamics are physically realistic.
\end{abstract}
