\section{Region Partition}
\label{sup:masks}

\paragraph{Derivation.}
For every generated sequence we average optical-flow magnitude over the leading
$12\%$ of sampled frame pairs and threshold it by Otsu's method into a dynamic
region and its complement. The static region is then reduced by a fixed
morphological erosion, and a $4\%$ frame border is discarded; together these
form the ignored transition band, so boundary pixels---where the partition is
least certain and flow least reliable---contribute to neither region. The same
procedure is applied to both tracks.

\paragraph{Why the estimate is restricted to an early window.}
Deriving a partition from a sequence's entire motion field would be circular,
since accumulated background drift would itself be read as evidence that the
affected region is dynamic. Restricting the estimate to the early window breaks
that circularity in the direction that matters: at the $60$\,s horizon the
partition is fixed from the first $7.1$\,s, before long-horizon drift has
accumulated, and the metrics are then evaluated over the remainder.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The partition is obtained automatically rather than by annotation, so no
inter-annotator agreement is reported, and for the image-conditioned track it is
derived per generated sequence rather than once from the shared source image.
The early window is a weaker guarantee than a first-frame annotation: a system
that drifts within the first $12\%$ of its rollout can influence its own
partition. We therefore treat partitions as measured quantities and report the
consequence directly---every measurement is recomputed under systematic erosion
and dilation of the boundary, with the resulting response given in
Table~\ref{tab:validation}. A human-verified, first-frame annotation protocol is
the natural strengthening of this component.

\section{Complete Validation Matrix}
\label{sup:validation}

Geometric severity is specified in a common physical unit---the mean pixel
displacement a corruption induces over the frame by the end of the rollout---and
each family's own parameter is derived from it using the frame geometry:
a translation of $d$ pixels, a rotation of $\arctan$-free angle
$\theta = d/\bar{r}$ radians, or a zoom of $1 + d/\bar{r}$, where $\bar{r}$ is
the mean distance from the frame centre. Expressing severity this way is what
makes the geometric families comparable; a parameter expressed in degrees is not
commensurate with one expressed in pixels, and treating them as though they were
understates angular corruptions by more than an order of magnitude at this
resolution.

\section{Complementary Perceptual Checks}
\label{sup:perceptual}

Neither check is aggregated into a factor, and neither is a preference study.
Both answer one question: does an independent judge, with no access to our
factors, order the same clip pairs the same way?

\paragraph{Pair selection.}
Pairs are chosen by rule rather than by eye. For each axis, candidate pairs of
systems on a shared prompt are binned by the \emph{relative gap} in the relevant
factor and sampled across three bands ($0.05$--$0.20$, $0.20$--$0.50$,
$0.50$--$1.00$), so agreement is measured over the whole operating range rather
than only where any metric would look good. Thirty pairs per axis are drawn.

\paragraph{Human study.}
Raters answer exactly two forced-choice questions---which video's background
moves more, and in which video the moving content slows or stops more---and are
never asked which video is better, since ``better'' conflates the two failures
the benchmark separates. Trial order and left/right presentation are randomised
per rater, choices are resolved back to system identities server-side, and
system names are never shown. Ties are permitted: forcing a choice on an
indistinguishable pair manufactures noise. Twenty-four trials per rater, three raters.

\paragraph{Vision--language judge.}
The same pairs, with the same wording, are put to an open-weight
vision--language model~\cite{qwen3vl2025}. Each pair is asked twice with the
clips swapped; a judge answering by position rather than by content disagrees
with itself, so agreement is computed only over self-consistent trials and the
self-consistency rate is reported beside it. A judge that contradicts itself on
a pair has expressed no opinion about that pair.

\paragraph{Reporting.}
Per axis we report the agreement rate with a binomial interval, the tie rate,
and agreement resolved by metric gap. The last matters: a factor that agrees
with observers only on large gaps is a weaker result than one that agrees
throughout, and pooling hides the difference.

\section{Signed Drift Attenuation}
\label{sup:dar}

Drift attenuation is stored as a signed quantity and reported clipped to
$[0,1]$. Negative values are informative rather than anomalous: where local flow
opposes the estimated global field, compensation increases the measured
magnitude. We report their incidence rather than suppressing it.
