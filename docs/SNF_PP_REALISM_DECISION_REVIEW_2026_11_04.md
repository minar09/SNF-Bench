# Critical decision review: realism and WACV response

2026-11-04. This review supplements `SNF_PP_REALISM_AND_REVIEW_PLAN_2026_11_04.md`; it does not approve new scoring, change a frozen metric, or turn an unfinished validation into a result.

## Decisions recommended

1. **Reuse:** recommend explicitly allowing new, versioned candidate statistics and derived controls on existing videos, while reusing existing scores and preserving all originals, manifests and frozen metric identities. This needs the user's authorization because of their earlier no-rescoring instruction. The already-run colour and paste pilots are exploratory development results; retain their records and disclose the deviation. They cannot be retrospectively designated untouched confirmation data. If reuse is declined, restrict new measurements and controls to new renders and independent real footage.
2. **Footage:** use both project-shot and appropriately licensed external fixed-camera recordings. A project member or paid local videographer must be assigned; no shooter is currently identified. Pilot 8–12 diverse independent scenes first to test capture and processing, then expand against a preregistered precision and coverage target. Four groups of 30 scenes is a starting budget, not a validation guarantee. Track creator, rights, location/session, duration, fps, stabilization, codec, medium, weather and relevant permissions. Separate collection sites/sessions and footage families before tuning.
3. **Masks:** recommend a separately versioned v2.1 subject-role design with two independent human annotators and an adjudicator. No annotator identities are established. Specify moving-subject instance and material as potentially overlapping semantic layers with visibility/occlusion and unknown labels, rather than assuming one mutually exclusive source-image pixel label captures future moving objects. Keep v2 unchanged. Update readers, schemas and compatibility checks before writing any new label: `scripts/snf_sourceref.py:load_role_masks` currently rejects label 4. Annotate direction separately with an unknown/ambiguous option.
4. **Human study:** recommend giving the existing study owner the question proposal below for integration, without changing their study or contacting them without an explicit instruction. Establish whether the existing instrument produces per-video scores before claiming video-level correlations.

## What the five checks can establish

These are hypotheses for distinct constructs, not five validated metrics. Preserve the existing independent score axes and report eligibility, uncertainty and coverage for each check. Predeclare target failures, negative cases, thresholds and appropriate baseline comparisons before held-out analysis.

| Check | Usable candidate and validation | Important limitation |
|---|---|---|
| R1 support integrity | Measure support changes, source fidelity and boundary coherence; compare paired controls with matched re-encodes and ask humans about visible background inconsistency. Include true fixed real backgrounds and coherent, deliberately composited examples. | Neither very low nor high change identifies a paste or redraw in general. A correct background can be still, moving lighting can be real, and a convincing composite may be indistinguishable. Do not report paste detection or compositing as a defect without evidence. |
| R2 intended moving material | Evaluate source material and subject roles separately with independently verified semantic labels. Test moving boat/frozen water and inverse cases, as well as occlusion and appearance of new objects. | Motion inside a mask alone cannot identify its material, and a source mask cannot automatically track a moving subject. Use output annotation where required; exclude or abstain if material or visibility is ambiguous. |
| R3 direction | Predeclare a scene-specific image-plane direction or sector; test matched-speed reversal, ping-pong, inflow, stationary and camera-motion controls. Report false direction and abstention rates separately. | Text-derived directions are candidate labels, not verified truth. Flow can be rotational, intermittent, turbulent or move in both directions. Perspective and changing visible texture confound flow and boundary flux; use unknown or multi-direction labels. |
| R4 repetition/progression | Evaluate recurrence together with reset/seam and freeze signals on exact, softened and variable loops; challenge them with real surf, fire, wind, periodic and nonperiodic negatives. | Recurrence alone cannot distinguish artificial looping from legitimate periodic dynamics, and smooth long loops can have no visible seam. Report detection and false-positive rates by medium and period; avoid claiming all natural repetition is ignored. |
| R5 motion plausibility | Compare candidate motion/texture features with matched real distributions and human judgments, with uncertainty and independent sites; compare track-based TRAJAN and set-level FVMD where applicable. | Matching real features does not establish physical truth, causal dynamics or correct semantics. Motion speed alone is not a universal defect: camera geometry, frame rate and material change its appearance. Speed controls are graded interventions whose perceptual validity needs independent labels. |

TRAJAN's track model supports single-video and distributional evaluation ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00209)); standard FVMD compares distributions of tracking-derived motion features ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16124)). Do not describe an FVMD between two small, unmatched sets as a per-video realism score; document sample-size sensitivity and scene-clustered uncertainty. [VBench-2.0](https://github.com/Vchitect/VBench/blob/master/VBench-2.0/README.md) provides multiple physics dimensions, not an automatic guarantee that its original prompts or scorer validate these nature scenes. Record any adaptation and coverage. As an additional incumbent for motion-related human alignment, review [VMBench](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Ling_VMBench_A_Benchmark_for_Perception-Aligned_Video_Motion_Generation_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf). Evaluate each comparator on the construct it actually claims to measure, with output direction and applicability declared; Dynamic Degree can respond to drift while rewarding it.

## Corrections to the review-coverage claims

* Source-fixed I2V masks remove the specific feedback loop in which each output's own early flow determines its mask. They do not establish semantic correctness, cover newly appearing subjects, or substitute for independent manual verification. T2V blinded output annotation remains open.
* The 96 T2V and 48 I2V scene specifications expand prompt coverage. They are not evidence that all methods, seeds, horizons, independent annotations or rights checks have been completed. A maximum of 12.5% for an individual regime does not imply equal shares for broader material groups: related water regimes must also be aggregated and reported.
* Same-scene specifications make a horizon comparison possible; completed matched rollouts and scoring are still required. Distinguish prefixes of the same long rollout from separately generated runs, and do not call the former independent videos.
* Precipitation assigned to an excluded overlay is an abstention for incompatible metrics, not a validated precipitation score. Report exclusions and avoid silently changing the population.
* The 24 fresh-source split is not automatically an untouched confirmation set if it or associated outputs were already inspected. Track exposure, and collect or reserve genuinely held-out scenes and model families before tuning. Verify rights for every source.
* The review's human-alignment concern remains open until relevant human data, reliability, correlations, uncertainty and independent confirmation exist. Repetition, realism, native-pipeline diversity and three seeds also remain open; proposed checks do not solve them by themselves.

## Practical gates and order

1. **Provenance first.** Inventory parents, existing scores, exploratory results and controls by hash. Protect historical artifacts; choose new output directories and fail rather than overwrite. Match each control and pass-through to the same parent, source, mask and sampling parameters, not merely a scene ID. Verify sidecars and decoded metadata including fps, timestamps, frame count and actual codec. A common encode history mitigates one confound, but content-dependent compression can still leak labels; test multiple codecs/settings and pixel-level artifacts on a prespecified subset.
2. **Fix pilot robustness before use.** In `pilot_support_realism.py`, missing sidecars are currently accepted, and only a present control's output hash is checked; require and verify the entire parent/source/mask/reference chain. The phase-correlation response is currently ignored; define failure/abstention criteria for low texture, nonfinite estimates and insufficient support, and avoid treating warp-border pixels as observed support. A translation plus global gray offset does not remove rotation, zoom or spatially varying light. Handle decoding and empty eroded masks explicitly and report actual sampling intervals. `build_composite_controls.py` currently writes into existing paths and assumes its encoding details; add no-clobber behavior and verify the resulting metadata. The six control modes have only a synthetic smoke test, not field validation.
3. **Develop protocols and independently annotated masks.** Lock source/role semantics, two independent raters, adjudication, a direction annotation guide, and an immutable split by source/site and model family. Review existing datasets' licenses and capture a small real-footage pilot. Real samples seen during development remain development data.
4. **Develop construct-specific controls and metrics.** Use known-answer tests and nuisance/invariance controls. Reuse existing footage for new candidate measurements only if the user authorizes it. Specify targets, units, direction, analysis, coverage, abstention and minimum practically meaningful effect before confirmation. No universal AUC requirement.
5. **Human pilot and precision planning.** Sample methods, scenes, media, horizons and failure severity; obtain independent blinded ratings and evaluate ordering, rater variation, task burden and uncertainty. Predeclare source-clustered and rater-aware analysis. Study real negatives and periodic phenomena as well as constructed controls. Choose main-study size based on pilot precision and intended comparisons; a fixed α ≥ 0.4 is not sufficient evidence of construct validity and can be unstable under low prevalence or skew. Report agreement and uncertainty, and investigate disagreement and inapplicability rather than treating the threshold as the sole gate.
6. **Render only missing cells** under recorded native settings, with seeds and horizon availability, avoiding duplicate existing videos. Evaluate previously scored outputs only under the authorization in decision 1. Use paired source-level comparisons, uncertainty clustered by independent source/site and model family, and separate source-conditioned I2V and T2V claims.
7. **Freeze and confirm.** Compare each eligible candidate against appropriate incumbent(s) on identical material, interventions and human labels. Preregister the incremental validity question, coverage and uncertainty; reserve untouched source and model families. Retain weak or selective checks as diagnostics and report adverse results. Publish the protocol and limitations without promising a journal outcome.

For independent Bernoulli scenes, zero observed false alarms in 30 gives an exact one-sided 95% upper bound of about 9.5%; zero in 59 gives about 5%. This is not guaranteed if recordings share sites or conditions, and 30 pooled across all media does not establish a 9.5% bound for every medium or periodic subtype. Choose sample sizes for the intended stratum and effect, accounting for clustering and missing/abstained cases.

## Proposal for the human-study owner (draft; not sent)

Please consider separate questions for (a) whether the requested material visibly keeps moving, including late windows; (b) whether the motion resembles the specified material; (c) whether its direction matches an unambiguous instruction; (d) noticeable loops, resets or late freezing; (e) whether the requested material, as opposed to another object, is moving; and (f) whether the background's edges, appearance and illumination fit coherently with the scene. Avoid presupposing that compositing is harmful. Use no-clear-difference only for pairwise questions and separate cannot-tell and not-applicable responses where relevant. Document whether ratings are pairwise or per-video; separate source-conditioned realism discrimination from preference and diagnostic defect ratings. Sample early, late and long-context views when studying 60–240 s behavior, as brief windows cannot establish whole-video progression. Confirm how many independent scene and rater clusters support each analysis and preserve the owner's existing protocol until jointly revised.
