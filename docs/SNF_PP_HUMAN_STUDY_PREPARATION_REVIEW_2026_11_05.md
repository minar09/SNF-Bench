# Human-study proposal: review and draft preparation

2026-11-05. This document is a draft for internal preparation, not an approved protocol. It does not change `human_study/`, any hosted media or links, and has not been sent to the study owner.

## Review of the preparation list

The preparation list correctly identifies a mismatch: `human_study/README.md` describes an I2V design while the local response export records T2V pairwise trials for drift and decay. The owner must establish the authoritative current protocol and the status of those responses. The eight listed IDs include a `test` ID; the number of usable independent raters is not yet established. Do not infer consent, completeness, eligibility or analytic independence from ID counts. The saved responses are not a human study of R1–R5.

The H1–H8 set is a reasonable starting point with the following revisions before a pilot:

* H1 mixes background movement and appearance change; specify whether geometry or either visible change is being rated, or separate them. A deliberately moving foreground should not count as background.
* The existing pairwise decay question and proposed single-clip H3 ask different things. Separate *motion amount late* from *slowing relative to early*, and do not merge v1 and new results as equivalent.
* H2 should ask about **unintended** disappearance, appearance or deformation; ordinary occlusion, water motion and smoke evolution are not automatically defects. An I2V source can aid identity but does not establish what should happen later; T2V needs its own prompt-based context.
* H4 is material-specific perceptual plausibility, not physical truth. H5 requires a separately verified and observable direction, and H7 may have several simultaneous moving materials; both need an explicit mixed/ambiguous option.
* H6 should distinguish perceptible exact/near replay or resets from ordinary repeated waves and vegetation. Unknown is a valid answer. Short windows alone do not establish absence of replay in a long video.
* H8 concerns visual coherence, not whether compositing occurred. Applicability, cannot-tell, and no-clear-difference have different meanings and must not be collapsed into one answer.
* The estimate of 12 one-minute clips in 30 minutes is only a workload hypothesis; loading, replays, instructions and eight answers can increase time. Estimate it with a timed pilot.

## Draft package items for the study owner

1. **Questions and response scale.** Keep H1–H8 as proposed constructs and apply the corrections above. Ask the owner to choose per-video ordinal questions where ratings are required, versus pairwise choices where comparative judgments are the target. For ordinal questions, use pre-piloted, question-specific verbal anchors; retain separate cannot-tell and not-applicable options. For pairwise trials offer A, B, no clear difference, cannot tell, and not applicable. Never derive an individual-video mean-opinion score from pairwise choices without a justified model and identifiable comparison graph.
2. **Playback.** For a proposed primary 60-second full-clip condition, document actual time, frame rate, dimensions, codec and whether replay is allowed. Treat longer full-clip and windowed conditions separately. If early/late windows are used, record exact timestamps and exclude claims about unobserved intervals. Pilot audio policy, network playback and accessible controls. Matching codec history reduces a confound but does not rule out compression cues.
3. **Design.** Randomize order and A/B position; blind system names, metric values and intervention identities. Use a balanced, connected pair graph for pairwise analyses; avoid showing the same source too often to a rater. Mix real footage, generator outputs and controls according to a preregistered plan, and use independently rights-cleared training examples outside confirmation. Repeated and identical-clip sentinels test consistency or attention, not validity by themselves. State exclusions before analysis.
4. **Burden.** Pilot time from first instruction to completion and report median and upper quantile, including replays and technical failures. Twelve 60-second videos already impose at least 12 minutes of playback; 30 minutes overall is unverified. Split sessions and use a balanced incomplete design if needed.
5. **Analysis.** Define each construct, primary metric and direction, eligible population, outcome, and primary comparison before confirmation. Keep pairwise preference, per-video ratings and real-versus-generated discrimination separate. Report counts and reasons for missing, cannot-tell, inapplicable and ties; test sensitivity to eligibility and exclusions. Use uncertainty that respects source/site and rater clustering, and model family where relevant. Report inter-rater agreement with intervals and prevalence context; no single reliability threshold proves validity. Compare appropriate incumbents on matched eligible data and report where a proposed metric adds no value.
6. **Blinding and allocation.** Create salted opaque stimulus and source IDs; retain the decoding key separately. Hide conditions and filenames from raters, randomize assignment with a saved seed, and keep source families, adjacent clips and interventions together in development/confirmation splits. Independent annotations of source roles and direction must not see metric outcomes.
7. **Proposed response fields.** `study_version`, `rater_pseudonym`, `session_id`, `trial_id`, `stimulus_a_id`, optional `stimulus_b_id`, `question_id`, `answer`, `eligibility_status`, `window_start_s`, `window_end_s`, `played_duration_s`, `replay_count`, `playback_problem`, `timestamp`, and `allocation_version`. Avoid collecting unnecessary identifying information; access and retention follow the owner's institutional requirements. The owner decides whether and how to implement these fields.

Next draftable items are a source-stratified sampling outline and a simulation-based precision plan with placeholder counts; direction eligibility, real anchors, consent, compensation and actual staffing remain open. No study should begin until the owner and institution decide the applicable consent and review process.
